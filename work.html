Illustrative example · Solo founder

A founder operating system without an operations department.

Hypothetical example only. No founder was interviewed, no client work is represented and no results are claimed.

Illustrative situation

A one-person professional services business handles client delivery, scheduling, invoicing follow-ups and marketing from scattered notes. The proposed assessment would first confirm what is actually happening.

Evidence to request

A founder interview, a walkthrough of one typical week and a redacted view of the current task and scheduling process.

Possible design, subject to evidence

A weekly priority review, one commitments tracker, a repeatable client-onboarding checklist and a decision log for changes to scope.

Acceptance checks

The founder can locate current commitments, identify the next action for each active project and complete the weekly review using the agreed template.

No time-savings estimate or improvement claim is made without a baseline.

Illustrative composite

Community nonprofit

12 staff, 60 volunteers · Stakeholder alignment and program management

+
Situation
A growing nonprofit running more programs each year with the same small staff and a large volunteer base.
Symptoms
Board, staff and volunteer leads each hold a different version of the program calendar. Decisions get reopened after meetings. Volunteers hear about changes late.
Diagnosis
No single owner for cross-program decisions, and no shared record of what was decided or why.
Design
A one-page decision-rights map, a monthly program review with a fixed agenda, a shared decision log, and a short volunteer update cadence.
What we'd measure
Decisions reopened per month, on-time program launches, volunteer no-show rate, and time leaders spend in coordination meetings.
Illustrative composite

Local retail and e-commerce shop

8 staff · Knowledge systems and operating cadence

+
Situation
An owner-led shop selling in store and online, where the owner is the answer to almost every question.
Symptoms
The same questions come up daily. New hires take weeks to get up to speed. Promotions launch with missing pieces.
Diagnosis
The operation runs on the owner's memory. Nothing recurring is written down, so every question routes back to one person.
Design
A short library of SOPs for the ten most repeated tasks, a 20-minute weekly team huddle, and a simple launch checklist for promotions.
What we'd measure
Questions routed to the owner per week, new-hire ramp time, and promotion launch errors.
Illustrative composite

Professional services practice

15 people · Leadership operating rhythm

+
Situation
A partner-led firm where two senior partners split their time between client work and running the practice.
Symptoms
Partners' calendars are full but strategic work keeps slipping. Internal meetings crowd out client time. Follow-ups fall through.
Diagnosis
There's no operating rhythm for the practice itself, so management work happens in the gaps between client meetings.
Design
A weekly partner meeting with a standing agenda, a quarterly priorities review, a decision and action log, and protected blocks for practice work.
What we'd measure
Hours of internal meetings per week, follow-ups closed on time, and progress on quarterly priorities.
Illustrative composite

Creative or marketing agency

20 people · AI adoption and capacity planning

+
Situation
An agency that bought several AI tools to speed up production and expected margins to improve.
Symptoms
A few people use the tools heavily, most don't, and nobody can say whether output or quality changed.
Diagnosis
The tools were treated as a purchase, not a change. There were no agreed use cases, training or measures of adoption.
Design
A readiness check, three pilot use cases with clear review rules, a short training plan, and a capacity view of where time actually goes.
What we'd measure
Weekly active users per tool, time saved on the pilot use cases, revision rounds per project, and team confidence.
