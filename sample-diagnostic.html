Sample deliverable

A diagnostic you can actually inspect.

A condensed findings memo for an illustrative nonprofit. It shows the structure of the work, not results from a real client.

Illustrative sample. The organization, findings and decisions below are hypothetical. No interviews were conducted and no performance results are claimed. A paid diagnostic would use agreed scope and actual evidence.

Scope

Team
A composite nonprofit with 12 staff and 60 volunteers.
Process
From proposed program date to a confirmed volunteer briefing.
Question
How can a date change reach the right people without reopening the decision in several meetings?

Evidence to collect

Two stakeholder interviews, a walkthrough of one recent date change and a redacted view of the current calendar and communication steps. Separate observed facts from assumptions before recommending changes.

Boundary: this sample assumes fragmented ownership. An actual diagnostic might find a different cause.

Process map

  1. Program lead proposes a date and checks capacity.
  2. Named approver decides, using the agreed criteria.
  3. Calendar owner records the approved date in one place.
  4. Communication owner briefs volunteers and records delivery.
  5. Program lead confirms readiness before launch.

Every handoff needs an owner, an input and a clear condition for moving forward.

Implementation and review

The diagnostic ends with a findings memo and a leadership readout. Implementation is a separately agreed scope. A first pilot could use one program, review exceptions weekly, and assess the agreed measures after 30 days before wider rollout.

Leaders confirm the proposed owners, approve the pilot and decide which baseline data is practical to collect. No savings estimate is made before the evidence supports one.
